Urbanyx (urbanyx.zaxis.ge/app) — UX Evaluation, Persona B: The Veteran

Persona: Zurab Kiknadze, 57. Owns a three-agent brokerage in Tbilisi, 22 years in property. High-value private houses in Vake/Saburtalo/Old Tbilisi, land in Mtskheta and Kakheti. Bifocals, no GIS background, will not read tooltips, will not scroll modals, delegates anything over three clicks. Looking for one reason to dismiss the tool — and one reason to keep it.

Method: task-based usability evaluation with think-aloud protocol. Session 2026-08-18, viewport ~859×939 CSS px (≈ the brief's 900 px case), signed in on a pre-existing Pro account. Font sizes and contrast ratios in this report are measured from the live DOM (computed styles, alpha-composited against the actual painted background), not estimated. Test-mode banner active throughout: «სატესტო რეჟიმი. გთხოვთ, არ შეიყვანოთ ბარათის მონაცემები გადახდის პორტალზე! უკუკავშირისთვის, მოგვწერეთ info@zaxis.ge».

Layers: persona reactions in quotes ("..."), evaluator observations in plain text.

Correction to the Persona A report (same product, 2026-08-17). Persona A's issue #1 stated that search accepts only cadastral codes and that place-name search fails. That was wrong. In this session place search worked repeatedly («ვაკე», «დიღომი», «ჭავჭავაძის გამზირი 37», «ვაჟა-ფშაველას გამზირი», «წინანდალი»). The real defect is worse and different: the geocoder answers confidently and often lands in the wrong place, with no statement of what it matched (§2, issue #1). Persona A's «ძიება წარუმატებელია» was a genuine failure but not the general rule.

1. Verdict

Usable by a veteran, low-tech, high-value agent today: NO.

  • The interface is physically too small for him. Nothing in the analysis panel is above 11.2 px; group headings are 8.8 px at 2.38:1 contrast, parcel-card labels («ფართობი», «მეპატრონე») 11.2 px at 3.48:1, inactive tabs 10.88 px at 3.11:1. WCAG AA needs 4.5:1. And the one coping strategy he'd use — browser zoom — breaks the layout: at 125% the parcel panel is clipped, at 150% every value is off-screen and only the labels column remains.
  • Search silently sends him to the wrong city. «ჭავჭავაძის გამზირი 62» landed in Telavi (parcel 53.08.40.611, «მუნიციპალიტეტი თელავი … კვარტალი 40»); «Chavchavadze Avenue Tbilisi» also landed in Telavi (53.20.39.117). Nothing on screen names the matched place. For a man whose business is knowing exactly which house is which, this is disqualifying.
  • The flagship number is inverted against market reality. Vake, Chavchavadze 62: 64 C. Dighomi Massive, Quarter IV korpusi 19: 69 C. Same analysis, same session, parcel-level both. A Kakheti vineyard scores 0 F. He will not use — or forgive — a system that rates Dighomi above Vake.
  • He cannot defend a single number. Of nine client-quotable numbers, only slope carries a source line and only the temperature layer carries a date (§8). There is no price, no valuation, no comparable anywhere: a DOM-wide scan for ფასი / ლარი / ₾ / GEL / USD / ღირებულებ / საბაზრო / price / valuation returned zero hits.
  • What would keep him: owner name + registration date in two clicks, and zoning coefficients turned into buildable m². That is real, and it is the only reason this report is not a flat "delete it".

2. The "tell me something I don't know" answer

Yes — three times, and they are all in the same place: the registry-plus-zoning layer, not the analytics. In two clicks the tool told him that the Dighomi block parcel belongs to a «ბინათმესაკუთრეთა ამხანაგობა» (homeowners' association), that the 981 m² strip beside Kazbegi 53 belongs to «სსიპ ქალაქ თბილისის მუნიციპალიტეტი» and was registered 14 აპრილი 2020, that a Telavi field is held by «თანამესაკუთრეები, სახელმწიფო», and — the genuinely new part — that this Saburtalo plot is 51% სარეკრეაციო ზონა-2 / 49% სსზ-1 with «მაქს. სიმაღლე 5 m», where the recreation zone's own tooltip computes «K1=0.20 → Max footprint 100 m², K3=0.80 → Min greening 402 m²». That is a "what can be built here" answer he currently gets by phoning an architect friend and waiting two days. It is worth money. But each of the three is undercut in the same session: the owner field was empty («—») on the Vake commercial parcel — exactly the kind he cares about most; the same zoning tooltip says «K2=0.00 → Max floor area 0 m², Max height (floors) 0» while the summary above it says max height 5 m; and no zoning number carries a plan version or date, which is precisely the thing a city hall would argue about. So: the product has a reason to exist for him. It is just not the reason it currently advertises on screen.

4. What works

  1. Owner + registration date + parcel type appear in two clicks, in plain Georgian, for parcels in Tbilisi and rural Kakheti (53.03.34.014, «თელავი , სოფელი წინანდალი», «თამარ ბერძენიშვილი», 6 დეკემბერი 2011).
  2. Zoning on a single parcel returns a share donut plus per-zone coefficient math in m² («Zone area: 502 m², K1=0.20 Max footprint 100 m²»).
  3. Search tolerates typos and Latin script — «ჭავჭავაზის გამზირი» and «Chavchavadze Avenue Tbilisi» both resolved without an error (accuracy is the separate problem, issue #1).
  4. Street-level 360° photos load automatically for the selected parcel, captioned «ქუჩის სურათები — ნაკვეთიდან 20 მეტრის მანძილზე».
  5. Nothing expired after 3 minutes idle: the selected parcel, panel and session were intact and interaction resumed normally.

Zurab's three readability judgements, without zooming:

  1. Is the body text comfortably readable? "არა. ნაკვეთის ბარათი წვრილია — სათვალით ვკითხულობ, მაგრამ იმ პატარა რუხ წარწერებს ვერ ვკითხულობ საერთოდ."
  2. Are the numbers on the map readable at default zoom? House numbers and street names on the base map are legible (dark teal on light). The numbers he actually needs are not on the map — they are in the 11.2 px panel. Parking/route lists wrap into 9–10 px grey runs.
  3. Is anything important rendered in light grey? Yes, and it is the labels rather than the values: «ფართობი / ტიპი / მისამართი / მეპატრონე / რეგისტრაციის თარიღი» at 3.48:1, the two inactive tabs at 3.11:1, and every section heading at 2.4–2.6:1. Interpretation: he can see the numbers but not what they are.

Other accessibility notes: the analysis panel is draggable but has no drag affordance; the panel exceeded the viewport bottom at this height with no visible scrollbar; the eight analysis controls are 48×48 px icon-only buttons with no text.

11. Structural problems

  • The product ranks places; he prices them. The livability index is an amenity-density measure presented as a grade. In Tbilisi, amenity density is highest in dense Soviet districts and lowest in the villa streets that cost the most, so the flagship metric is negatively correlated with the thing his business runs on. Measured, not asserted: Vake 64 C vs Dighomi Massive 69 C, and paid parking counted as a penalty in the district where paid parking is a symptom of demand.
  • It is a session tool; he needs an answer tool. Everything valuable is behind a stack of toggles inside one draggable panel that must stay open, in a session with no URL, no link, no print, and no state after reload. His actual unit of work is "one parcel, one page, on paper, in front of a seller".
  • "Urban" analytics applied to farmland. A Kakheti vineyard receives «URBAN LIVABILITY INDEX 0 F». For land — 40% of his business — the tool has the cadastral layer he wants and an analytics layer that grades the land as a failure for not having a bus stop. Nothing tells him the metric doesn't apply.
  • Coverage is undeclared at the point of use. Zoning silently returns nothing outside Tbilisi; the pricing table says relief is Tbilisi-only while relief in fact computed in Kakheti and cited a Tbilisi DEM as its source. He cannot learn where the tool is trustworthy.
  • Registry text is passed through raw. «ქალაქი თბილისის , გამზირი ი.ჭავჭავაძე , N 62» is a database concatenation, not an address. To a Georgian professional this reads as "nobody who speaks my language checked this screen", which colours everything else on it.
